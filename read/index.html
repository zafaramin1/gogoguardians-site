A note from the author

Preface

~ ◆ ~

I grew up hearing stories about Alibaba and the Forty Thieves. I didn't fully understand them, but there was always humor and enchantment in them. They felt magical.

Years later, I started telling bedtime stories to my own three children, Sariah, Yusuf and Sabrina, and Alibaba and the Forty Thieves became their favorite. I made up my own version as I went along. Even I didn't know how each story would end, but it always sorted itself out, usually right about the time they got sleepy.

They were my first audience and my biggest supporters. They laughed so loud that telling the story was easy. Honestly, I loved those nights as much as they did.

Seeing their reaction, I thought, why not write a book? So I did, and I'm really glad I have.

I wanted to bring that magic back through the one thing kids understand best: their phone. Not just kids, really. All of us.

You would think technology would give us more imagination, but to me it often feels like less. Things feel flat. There is still wonderful imagination out there, in good books and good movies, but I don't find enough of the simple wonder those old stories had before technology. This book is my way of connecting today's world back to it.

That's why I put so much emphasis on the pictures. Imagery matters, and not just for children. It brings a fantasy to life, and I worked hard on it.

Above all, this book is meant to be fun. It's entertainment and fantasy, a chance to go away to another world for a while. I hope you enjoy the journey.

Zafar Amin

Prologue

Hidden Wonders of Sumner County

~ ◆ ~

In the small town of Sumner County, Kansas, something amazing is starting to happen, but no one has noticed yet. It's a story that mixes everyday life with bits of the unexpected and exciting. It's about to come to life in the most surprising ways and places. Our story kicks off in a place everyone knows: a home where a grandmother's deep love for her grandson, Oliver, sparks an adventure that no one saw coming. Oliver's life, filled with the usual school days and joys of being a kid, is about to bump into something very out of the ordinary. Life is full of surprises, especially the big, life-changing ones that tend to find us when we least expect them.

Right under the surface of Oliver's normal life is a hint of something much bigger: a promise of an adventure, a family secret that's way more exciting than anything ordinary. Here, a simple birthday gift is the first step into a journey into the unknown, one that will change Oliver’s life and show him a world he never knew existed.

As we start this story, we follow Oliver in his regular life, but soon he's stepping into a family story that's been passed down quietly for generations. His journey is going to stretch his imagination, create new friendships, and lead him to uncover mysteries and seek out adventures.

This story is about finding the magic in our everyday lives and the adventurous spirit that lives in all of us. It's a reminder that the most incredible stories are often hiding right where we live, just waiting for someone like Oliver to find them. Our world is full of surprises, and things are often not what they seem. Just like how looking at something under a microscope can show us a whole new side to it, this story will show us there’s always something more to discover.

Welcome to a world where magic is a subtle yet constant friend, where surprises and adventures are around every corner, and where a young boy's journey is about to uncover the amazing things that lie just beyond our everyday perception.

Chapter 1

The Midnight Glow

~ ◆ ~

Grandma walked the aisles of the electronics store in Sumner County, Kansas, with one mission: find Oliver the perfect thirteenth birthday present. Not a nice present. The perfect one. Something he would remember for years to come.

She passed the speakers, the tablets and the game controllers, each one fancier than the last. Then she stopped. On the display stand sat a brand-new iPhone, sleek and shining.

Grandma had reached a point in her life where her own needs were simple. These days, her greatest joy was giving, especially to her grandchildren. There was something deeply fulfilling about finding a gift that would not just make them happy, but open new doors for them.

She picked up the phone and turned it over in her hands. A whole world in a box this small, she thought. Maps, music, every book ever written, every corner of the planet. A way to learn, to connect, to explore far beyond the borders of their small town. She pictured Oliver's face when he opened it, and she smiled.

Today was not a day for being careful with money. A gift like this didn't come along every day, and she wanted this birthday to be one Oliver would never forget. Grandma's heart swelled. She carried the phone to the checkout as if it were made of glass, already imagining Oliver tearing off the wrapping paper.

That evening, after a busy day out, she returned to her cozy house and set the wrapped box on her bedroom dresser. The house was quiet except for the old hallway clock, ticking away like it always had.

Grandma and Grandpa Henry got ready for bed, changing into their pajamas in the same comfortable routine they had shared for so many years. Grandpa Henry took off his glasses and nodded at the box. "Oliver's going to be over the moon," he said with a soft chuckle. "I can't wait to see his face."

"I hope so." Grandma fluffed her pillow. "It's quite a present for a thirteen-year-old, isn't it? When we were kids, a phone like that would have been science fiction."

"The world changes fast," Grandpa said, thinking back on the good old days. "One little gadget, and the whole world fits in your pocket."

"Imagine," Grandma said. "Such a small thing, and it can open up whole new worlds for him."

She switched off the lamp, and darkness filled the room. They kissed goodnight and settled in.

Hours later, in the middle of the night, the box began to glow.

It started as a faint shimmer, then grew brighter, a soft golden light shining straight through the wrapping paper. It spread across the dresser, the walls, the ceiling, until the whole room was bathed in gold.

Grandma woke first. She nudged her husband. "Henry. Henry, look."

Grandpa sat up, rubbing his eyes. "Did it turn itself on?" He squinted. "But light doesn't come through cardboard. That's not normal."

They watched, puzzled and amazed. The night that had been so quiet and dark was now alive with golden light. They should have been frightened. Instead, a strange calm settled over them, warm as a blanket. They felt safer than they had in years, and neither of them could say why.

Then Grandma's eyes went wide. "I know what this is," she whispered. "I know."

"Know what? And how could you possibly know?"

"I've known this day would come for a long time. And now it's here."

"So what is it?" Grandpa said. "Are you going to leave me hanging?"

"It's Elderstone."

"Elderstone?" Grandpa said. "What on earth is..."

BANG, BANG, BANG. Someone was pounding on the front door downstairs.

They both froze.

Grandpa Henry crept down the stairs, his heart racing, his mind filling with every terrible thing it could be. At the bottom, he took a breath and opened the door a crack.

It was their neighbor, Gladys, standing on the porch in her old cardigan, twisting a handkerchief in her hands, tears running down her cheeks. "Have you seen Cleo?" she sobbed, her voice trembling. "I've been looking for him all night."

Grandpa let out a long breath. Just Gladys. Just her cat. Not some danger coming for them. He put a gentle hand on her shoulder. "Don't worry, Gladys. We'll help you find him. He's probably hiding somewhere close."

Upstairs, behind him, the golden glow slowly faded, and the box on the dresser went dark, as if it had been waiting for something, and that something was not tonight.

Chapter 2

Birthday Buzz

~ ◆ ~

With Oliver's thirteenth birthday only days away, the Wilderidge house was buzzing.

Oliver couldn't sit still. His messy chestnut hair stuck up in every direction, and his green eyes darted around as if an adventure might be hiding behind the couch.

"Slow down, space ranger," said Jack, his fifteen-year-old brother, as Oliver zoomed past for the third time. Jack was the calm one. He watched the world first and jumped in second.

"Leave him alone," said Lily, their eleven-year-old sister, her blonde curls bouncing. "He's happy. And it's almost his birthday, so we can start celebrating now. Those are the rules."

"Those are not the rules," Jack said.

"They are now."

At breakfast, Dad leaned back in his chair with his coffee. He was a mechanic who could fix anything with an engine, and he still smelled faintly of motor oil even on his days off. "So, birthday boy. What's the plan?"

Oliver rattled it off: water guns, pizza, his best friends, cake. "And Grandma's present," he added. "She said it's a big one. A really big one."

"Maybe it's a ticket to space!" Lily said. "Or a robot!"

"They don't make good enough robots yet," Oliver said. "If they did, yeah, that would be awesome."

"Coming soon," Jack said, without looking up from his cereal.

Dad grinned. "With Grandma, you never know. She lives for days like this."

"Riddle of the day," Dad announced, the way he did every morning. "What has to be broken before you can use it?"

"A phone," Lily said.

"An egg," said Jack, pointing at his plate.

"Correct," Dad said. "Lily, please don't break any phones."

Mom set down a plate of eggs. She was a teacher, and she had a way of saying important things in a soft voice that somehow made you listen harder. "Thirteen," she said, shaking her head. "My little baby is growing up."

"Hey," Lily said. "I'm the baby."

"You're all my babies," Mom said. "Even the ones who are about to be teenagers." She looked at Oliver. "Just remember, look before you jump."

"I always look before I jump," Oliver said.

Jack snorted. Lily giggled. Even Dad hid a smile behind his mug.

"Mostly," Oliver admitted.

That evening, the phone rang. Lily got to it first, because Lily always got to it first.

"Grandma! Is it a robot? Just tell me if it's a robot. I won't tell Oliver."

Oliver lunged for the phone. Lily ducked under the kitchen table with it. There was a lot of shrieking.

When he finally wrestled it away, Grandma was laughing on the other end. "Hello, birthday boy."

"Grandma, what is it? Please. Just a hint."

There was a pause, and when Grandma spoke again her voice was different. Softer. More careful. "It's something very special, Oliver. Something that has been in our family for a long, long time, in one way or another." Another pause. "Promise me you'll take good care of it."

"I promise," Oliver said, confused. "But what do you mean, in our family? You just bought it, right?"

"Goodnight, sweetheart," said Grandma. "Sleep well. Big day coming."

Oliver hung up and stood in the kitchen for a long time, frowning at the phone.

"What did she say?" Jack asked.

"She said it's been in our family for a long time."

Jack shrugged. "Grandmas say weird stuff."

But that night, Oliver lay awake listening to the house creak, and he couldn't stop thinking about the way Grandma's voice had changed. As if she knew something. As if she were a little bit afraid.

Chapter 3

The Mysterious Gift Unveiled

~ ◆ ~

The doorbell started ringing at noon and didn't stop.

"Happy birthday, Oliver!" Ethan shoved a wrapped box into his hands. "When do the water guns come out?"

"Right now. We've got work to do in the backyard."

Hannah came next, holding a card covered in her own drawings: Oliver on a rocket, Oliver riding a dragon, Oliver on top of a mountain. "Sorry it's not a real present," she said.

Oliver stared at it. "Are you kidding? This is better than a real present."

Grace arrived with a tiny, mysterious box. "Bet you can't guess what's inside."

"You're right," Oliver laughed. "I'm terrible at guessing."

Soon the backyard was full: twelve friends, plus Oliver, Jack and Lily. Lily made sure every single person had a party hat, including herself. Dad ran the grill. Mom ran everything else.

"Everybody showed up," Jack told Lily as they set out the cups. "He was worried for nothing."

"I love birthdays," Lily said. "Mostly for the cake."

Dad organized a three-legged race across the lawn, which Oliver and Ethan lost by falling into the rosebush. Grace won the sack race. Hannah drew everyone's portrait on paper plates, and they were so good that the parents started asking her to do theirs. Lily ran around refilling cups of lemonade whether anyone wanted them or not.

Grandma sat in a lawn chair in the shade, watching all of it with a cup of tea, and every so often her eyes drifted to the small shimmering box on the gift table, as if she were keeping an eye on it.

When it was time for presents, everyone crowded around. Chris gave him a chess set. "Hope you're good at chess," Chris said, "because I am."

"I can play," Oliver said. "I just can't promise I'll win."

But the whole time, Oliver's eyes kept sliding to one small box wrapped in shimmering paper. Grandma's. It almost seemed to be calling him.

At last he tore the paper away.

"An iPhone!" his friends shouted.

"It's an iPhone," Oliver said, holding it up. Then he went quiet. The phone felt warm in his hands. Not hot, just warm, like something alive. And it was humming, so softly that only he could feel it.

"Turn it on!" Jack said.

Oliver pressed the button. The screen burst into swirling colors, gold and green and deep blue, and every kid leaned in.

"I've never seen one do that," Ethan said. "You're so lucky."

Oliver looked across the yard. Grandma was watching him with a small, knowing smile. He ran over and threw his arms around her. "Thank you, Grandma. This is amazing."

"Anything for you, my dear," she said, and held him a moment longer than usual. "Happy birthday."

It was the most expensive thing anyone had ever given him. But that wasn't what made it special. What made it special was that Grandma had picked it, just for him.

When he got back to his friends, a new app had appeared on the screen all by itself. A golden icon. Underneath it, one word: Gogo.

Oliver tapped it.

A map filled the screen, but not of Kansas, or of anywhere he had ever seen. Strange mountains. A city with domes and towers. An endless golden desert. And for one second, on the edge of the desert, a tall man in a blue turban turned and looked straight out of the screen at Oliver. He winked. Then he was gone.

Oliver nearly dropped the phone.

"I've seen a lot of maps," Grace said slowly, "and none of them look like that."

"Maybe it's a clue," Hannah whispered. "To some kind of adventure."

"An adventure?" Oliver said. "I'm ready."

Then Ethan blasted him in the back with a water gun, and the whole party exploded into a soaking, screaming, laughing water fight. By the time Mom carried out the cake with thirteen candles glowing on top, every towel in the house was wet.

That night, after the last guest had gone home, Dad helped him carry his presents upstairs. "That's quite a haul, birthday boy."

"Yeah," Oliver said. But he was only holding one thing. "Dad, it doesn't feel like a phone. It feels like it's meant for something."

From the hallway, Mom called, "Whatever it's meant for, take good care of it. That's a very expensive present for a thirteen-year-old!"

Oliver climbed into bed and set Gogo on his nightstand. It glowed softly in the dark.

He thought about the strange map. He thought about the man in the blue turban who had winked at him.

"Who are you?" he whispered.

The screen flickered once, as if it were thinking about the answer.
